# Account A's idle cash in SPY: the open's buys were counted twice – 2026-11-23

**Found:** on Monday 23 Nov (SPY +4.71%) A's paper equity was $803 above Alpaca's. The stocks matched (their
gain today: $1,969 in the paper, about $1,960 at Alpaca). The gap was in SPY.

**Cause:** the paper's equity line (`runner.with_idle_spy`) gave SPY a weight of 1 minus the trades' exposure at
the previous close. Stocks bought at the next open are not in that exposure yet, so the cash spent on them stayed
"in SPY" for the whole day while it was also in the stocks. The plan (orders.json) and Alpaca never did that: the
plan keeps the cash for the next open's buys out of SPY. On 23 Nov A bought about $10,000 of ELV, TJX and WMB at
the open after SPY had gapped up 5% overnight: about $460 of the gap.

**Fix:** the day's SPY weight now also leaves out what was bought at that day's open, as a share of the previous
close's equity (`runner.bought_at_open`). Tests: tests/test_runner.py.

**Effect on A's record (A re-run both ways on 23 Nov):**
- the stock orders are identical;
- the recorded equity is lower on most days, by $15 to $307, and higher on 20 Nov, when SPY fell 3.6% on the
  day PLD was bought; on 23 Nov $88,581 instead of $88,907 (-$326, -0.37%);
- the SPY holding is sized from the corrected equity: 26.72 shares instead of 27.17.

**What remains between the paper and Alpaca, and is real:** cash freed by a stop reaches SPY in the paper at
that day's close but at Alpaca only the next morning at 9:50 (about $345 on 23 Nov, after Friday's seven stops
and SPY's +5% weekend gap), and fills differ from the paper's prices (about -2R since 23 Oct).

**The research behind A** (reports/2026-10-20-insider-spy) used the same formula. Re-running it needs the full
insider history, which lives in the GitHub Actions cache (this container does not download from the SEC), so
here is an estimate instead. A buys on average 6.7% of its equity at the open each day (42 entries in 21
sessions). SPY earned about 11% a year over the research years, so the double count added roughly
0.067 x 11% = **about 0.7 points a year** to "insider trades + SPY", and a little volatility. The research found
"about 4 points a year more than SPY"; corrected, roughly 3.3 points a year, still mostly because the account is
more than fully invested on average. The conclusion it drew ("a small, honest SPY plus a little, not a clear win")
stands, a little smaller. The re-run can be done on GitHub at the 6 Dec review.
