# News: what the feed was worth, and a proper test (14 November 2026)

The user's question (12 November): Nvidia fell on news for two weeks and no account shorted it; is the
news reading broken? The user's go (14 November): "fix it and make it useful for some account".

## Summary

- **The daily news feed (GDELT) was broken since 30 September.** It is fixed, but the source itself refuses
  most requests and has holes.
- **On the data we have, news did not predict the next day.** Over 18,000 to 20,000 asset-days, neither the
  daily tone nor the headline scores said anything usable about what came next. Headlines describe the move
  that already happened.
- **So no account gets a news rule today.** A rule without evidence would be decoration.
- **A proper test starts now:** news for every S&P 1500 stock since 2015, three ideas written down below
  before any result, scored by the finder's 14 checks like every other strategy. If one of them holds, it
  is a candidate for an account at the 6 December review.

## What was broken

- The system asks GDELT (a free news database) for a daily article count and tone for 8 assets.
- GDELT refuses most requests from shared machines (HTTP 429). On 14 November it refused 3 of 4 requests
  from this session, and what it returned had holes: 33 days out of 50.
- **The bug:** the first refusal in a run stopped the run from asking for any other asset. Only the first
  asset (BTC) was ever refreshed; the other seven stayed at 30 September.
- The morning routine also discarded whatever it had refreshed, so the repo's copies never moved.
- No live account read this feed after 9 November (C left the hourly system). It fed the research book
  and the learner.

**Fixed** (tests first): one refusal no longer stops the others (three in a row do, so a run does not wait
on a dead service); a day GDELT has no article for has no tone instead of a neutral one; the trading
workflow saves the refreshed files to the repo once a day.

## What the data say

Close to close, each asset's own average return taken out.

**A. GDELT daily tone, as the system used it** (7 assets, July 2017 to September 2026, 18,137 asset-days):

| signal | days ahead | rank correlation | lowest fifth | highest fifth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tone | 1 | +0.003 | -0.9 bp | -0.4 bp |
| tone | 5 | +0.003 | +4.5 bp | +1.0 bp |
| coverage | 1 | -0.003 | -1.7 bp | -1.5 bp |
| coverage | 5 | +0.006 | -3.6 bp | +30.6 bp |

Nothing. The monthly t-statistics are between -1.3 and -2.2: if anything, slightly the wrong way.

**B. Headline tone, Alpaca / Benzinga** (22 assets of the hourly system, February 2015 to November 2026,
20,024 asset-days with at least one article; articles up to the close, move of the next session):

| signal | days ahead | rank correlation | t (by month) | lowest fifth | highest fifth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tone of the day | 1 | -0.017 | -2.6 | +7.5 bp | +1.7 bp |
| tone of the day | 5 | -0.020 | -3.5 | +20.2 bp | +0.9 bp |
| number of articles vs usual | 5 | +0.023 | +2.7 | -9.8 bp | +46.3 bp |

- **The same day's move: +0.132.** Headlines follow the price; they are written about a move, not before it.
- **After a clearly bad-news day** (676 days: tone below -0.3 on 3 or more articles) the next day was 7 bp
  *better* than the asset's average (t +0.6) and the next five days 37 bp better (t +0.6). A rule
  "short after bad news" would have lost a little on average.
- After a clearly good-news day (5,273 days): -4 bp the next day (t -1.1).
- **Nvidia, 30 October to 13 November:** eleven sessions, eight down (-4.2%, -1.3%, -3.2%, -9.3%, -4.3%,
  -5.5%, -4.3%, -2.3%), about -24% in all. The headline tone was negative on 6 of them and positive on 5.
  In hindsight a clear slide; in eleven years of these assets, not a pattern that repeats.

**Limits of this evidence:**

- 22 hand-picked large stocks and funds that did well: not a fair universe.
- The scorer counts good and bad words in a headline. It misses most of what a reader sees.
- Daily bars only. Whatever news does within minutes is not measured here, and is not something a daily
  paper account can trade.
- The two faint effects (a small reversal after bad headlines, higher returns after busy news days) come
  from this biased universe. They are hypotheses, not findings.

## The test (written before any result)

**Data:** every article of Alpaca's archive since January 2015, about 2.8 million. Per stock and day, no
text kept: articles about the company (tagged with at most 3 stocks), how many read as good or bad news,
and round-ups that mention it among many (algo/wide_news.py). An article after the 16:00 close counts for
the next day. About ten hours of download on GitHub, then a few minutes a night.

**Three ideas, S&P 1500, the members of each day, 8 versions each (24 tries):**

| family | buys at the close when | exits tested | where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| news_quiet_drop | a stock falls 2 or 3 times its usual daily move and no article about it appeared in the last 3 or 10 sessions | 5 or 10 days | moves without news reverse (Chan, 2003) |
| news_bad_day | it falls that much on 1+ or 3+ articles, more bad than good, outside earnings | 5 or 10 days | the small reversal in table B |
| news_good_day | it rises that much on 1+ or 3+ articles, more good than bad, outside earnings | 20 days, or a trailing stop up to 60 | drift after news |

- A stock counts only while the news covers it at all (an article in the last 250 sessions): otherwise
  "no news" cannot be told from "no data".
- Earnings days are left out of the last two: the reaction to earnings has its own families, already tried.
- Nothing is scored until every month is downloaded: a missing month would read as silence.

**What would make news useful for an account:**

- A family that passes the checks the insider rules passed (13 of 14) becomes a candidate for an account at
  the 6 December review. It would be the first rule in the accounts that is not insider buying.
- **A clearly negative result is useful too.** If stocks that fall on bad headlines keep falling
  (news_bad_day with a clearly negative edge), that is the tested bearish signal account F's bearish option
  plays have been waiting for.
- If all three look like chance, the answer is that daily headlines carry nothing these accounts can trade,
  and the news inputs stay out of every account.

## Results (15 November 2026)

The download finished on 14 November (143 months, January 2015 to now). The nightly finder scored the 24
versions on 15 November: search 2017-2022, validation 2023 to mid-2025, the members of each day.

**None of the three ideas works. The best versions pass 9 of 14 checks, and all 24 have a negative
t-statistic once the market is taken out (-0.26 to -4.08; about 4.6 is needed).**

| family | checks | signals 2017-22 | average trade vs random entries | return 2017-22 | return 2023-25 | t without the market |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| news_quiet_drop | 5 to 7 | 6,560 to 37,076 | -0.08R to 0.00R vs -0.03R to 0.00R | -34% to -79% | -12% to -37% | -1.9 to -4.1 |
| news_bad_day | 6 to 9 | 597 to 3,021 | -0.03R to +0.06R vs -0.05R to +0.09R | -22% to +22% | -12% to +2% | -0.3 to -2.4 |
| news_good_day | 1 to 3 | 973 to 5,644 | -0.03R to +0.05R vs +0.03R to +0.11R | -16% to +28% | -13% to +10% | -0.5 to -1.9 |

**What the data say:**

- **After a big drop, with or without news, a stock does bounce a little.** Over the next 5 to 10 days it
  gains 0.1 to 0.27 of a typical day's range more than it usually does, in both periods. That is a few
  tenths of a percent: less than the stops and the trading costs take. The trades lose (buying quiet
  drops: -34% to -79% over 2017-2022).
- **After a big rise on good headlines, a stock does worse than usual** over the next one to three months,
  in both periods and in all 8 versions. There is no drift to ride: buying them was worse than buying at
  random in every version.
- **No bearish signal for account F.** Stocks that fall on bad headlines do not keep falling; they bounce
  slightly, on 1,500 stocks as on the 22 of the first check. The lag after good-news jumps is a stock
  rising more slowly than usual, not a stock falling: too small to bet against.

**Conclusion:** daily headlines, read by a word list, give these accounts nothing to trade. News stays out of
every account. The finder keeps refining news_bad_day on its own (its steadiness is above the bar for that),
at the cost of a few more tries; the other two are dropped by its own rules.

**Limits:** the scorer is a word list; finer reading of the text might find more. Moves within the day are
not measured. The vault period (from July 2025) was not touched.
